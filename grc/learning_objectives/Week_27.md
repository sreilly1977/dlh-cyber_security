## Security Awareness & Human Defense

---

### Technical

**Q: What does a phishing simulation campaign design include?**

**A:** A targeting strategy mapped to role-based risk, staged wave scheduling, and behavioral metrics like report rate and click-to-report conversion that track measurable behavior change over time rather than one-off performance.

**Q: What makes effective healthcare-sector social engineering lures?**

**A:** Realistic pretexts drawn from healthcare threat patterns (credential reset, EHR access, payroll, medical supply fraud) calibrated to each role's actual workflow, terminology and pressure points.

**Q: What does a vishing simulation framework contain?**

**A:** Complete scenario scripts with caller personas and branching pretexts, structured assessment rubrics (info disclosed, escalation behavior), and debrief protocols covering immediate coaching and referral paths.

---

### Conceptual

**Q: What distinguishes behavior-measuring from awareness-measuring campaigns?**

**A:** Behavior-measuring campaigns test what people actually do when deceived in-workflow (click, disclose, report), while awareness-measuring campaigns only test what people say they know, which famously diverges from real conduct.

**Q: How does behavior change theory shape training design?**

**A:** Spaced reinforcement beats one-off sessions, just-in-time coaching at the moment of failure converts mistakes into learning, and role-targeted content ensures relevance, since behavior change requires repetition, timely feedback and personal applicability.

**Q: What are the ethical boundaries of security simulation?**

**A:** Exclusion criteria protecting vulnerable staff (recent trauma, performance concerns), leadership-approved consent frameworks acknowledged during onboarding, and firm post-simulation support obligations, meaning no public shaming, confidential results, and resources for anyone distressed.

# Transversal

**Q: How do you write security guidance people actually use?**

**A:** Keep it short, plain-language, embedded in existing workflows (banner tips, popup nudges) rather than separate documents, with concrete tell-me-what-to-do instructions instead of policy language.

**Q: How do you design insider threat criteria without surveillance overreach?**

**A:** Focus on protective indicators tied to observable, job-relevant events (failed access escalations, off-hours bulk downloads, policy-triggered alerts) with defined thresholds, documented governance and privacy review, never open-ended monitoring of personal behavior.

**Q: How do you translate program metrics for executives and boards?**

**A:** Convert click and report rates into risk-reduction narrative (fewer incidents, lower breach likelihood, cost avoided), benchmark against sector norms, and tie results directly to budget line items for continued training and simulation investment.
