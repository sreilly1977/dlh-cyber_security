## Your biggest threats

- **Fake "remote access" emails.** Messages claiming your VPN or MFA enrollment expires today and must be completed through a link. It targets you because your connection to MedDefense *is* a login process, so a message about fixing it feels routine. **Watch for:** any remote-access task that arrives by email link instead of the portal you normally use.

- **Public-space exposure.** Screens read over your shoulder on trains and in cafés, confidential calls taken within earshot, work sessions on open WiFi where credentials and documents can be intercepted. **Watch for:** anyone lingering behind you when you're working with patient, billing, or HR material — and any WiFi that asks you to install something to connect.

- **Work flowing into personal tools.** Moving files into personal cloud storage for convenience, or forwarding work email to a personal account so you can check it on a personal device. Once it leaves MedDefense systems, none of MedDefense's protections follow it. **Watch for:** the moment you think "I'll just grab this from my personal account tonight."

## What to do right now

- Connect VPN **before** opening any work system, every session — then check that it's actually connected before logging into email or the portal.
- Change your home WiFi admin password if it is still the router default, and confirm you are on WPA2/WPA3 with a password only your household knows.
- Pick one device rule and keep it: work documents live on your work machine and in MedDefense systems, full stop. If sharing is needed, use MedDefense-approved sharing, not personal cloud or personal email.

## When something looks wrong

Click the **Report Phishing** button in Outlook, or email security@meddefense.org. If you entered your password somewhere and it felt wrong after, call **ext. 4-SECURITY** right away — fast reporting after a mistake is the most valuable thing you can do, and it is never treated as a disciplinary event.

## What not to do

- Don't use personal email or personal cloud storage for work files, even temporarily, even for convenience.
- Don't work on patient, billing, or personnel documents where strangers can see your screen or hear your calls.
- Don't trust a "certificate" or "security update" prompt that appears while browsing — close the tab and reconnect VPN instead.
