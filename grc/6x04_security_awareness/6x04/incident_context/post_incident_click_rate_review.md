**Review owner:** Sarah Park and SOC Metrics Team  
**Date:** 2026-04-12  
**Classification:** Confidential

## Observed Behaviors

### Click Behavior

- Most clinical clicks occurred within 20 minutes of delivery.
- Clinical users clicked fastest during shift-start windows.
- Billing users clicked when the message referenced compliance or portal access.
- Finance users opened messages more often than they clicked, but were more likely to reply or consider offline action.
- IT users were suspicious of generic lures but responded to vendor-support framing.

### Reporting Behavior

- Reporting was delayed even among users who suspected something was wrong.
- Some employees verbally told coworkers but did not use the report button.
- Several employees believed reporting would get someone "in trouble."
- No department had a consistent expectation for what happens after reporting.

## Employee Feedback Themes

| Theme | Representative Feedback |
|---|---|
| Fear of losing access | "If Epic access is blocked, I cannot do my job." |
| Unclear reporting path | "I wasn't sure whether to call IT or forward it." |
| Fear of blame | "I did not want to report it after I clicked." |
| Normalized urgency | "Everything in clinical work feels urgent." |
| Executive pressure | "If a director asks for something, you don't want to slow them down." |

## Awareness Program Requirements

1. Train employees that reporting after clicking is still valuable.
2. Reinforce that simulation results are not performance review data.
3. Give managers aggregate data only.
4. Provide role-specific examples.
5. Make reporting feedback immediate.
6. Teach verification behaviors, not just red flags.
7. Track report rate and mean time to report, not only click rate.

## HR Boundary

Individual click results must not be shared with department managers unless there is a second confirmed offense and formal HR escalation. Managers receive aggregate team trends.
