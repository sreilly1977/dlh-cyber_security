# Simulation Ethics Guidelines

**Approved by:** HR and IT Security draft  
**Purpose:** Define boundaries for phishing, vishing and insider-threat awareness simulations.

## Ethical Principles

1. Train behavior, do not humiliate.
2. Use realistic scenarios without exploiting trauma or personal hardship.
3. Protect individual results.
4. Provide immediate learning after failure.
5. Reward reporting.
6. Avoid patient-care disruption.
7. Use HR-reviewed escalation for repeat patterns.

## Prohibited Simulation Themes

Do not use lures based on:

- personal medical diagnoses
- death or severe personal emergencies
- immigration status
- payroll termination threats
- disciplinary threats
- protected class attributes
- real patient names or real patient conditions
- active disasters or recent traumatic events
- explicit fear that employment will immediately end

## Permitted High-Realism Themes

Allowed with approval:

- Epic account verification
- MFA enrollment
- benefits open enrollment
- secure document review
- vendor support callback
- invoice or payment verification
- remote-work compliance
- travel security notice

## Approval Requirements

| Artifact | Required Approval |
|---|---|
| Threat profile | CISO |
| Phishing templates | CISO + HR |
| Vishing scripts | CISO + HR + Legal if recorded |
| Insider threat manager guide | HR + Legal |
| Campaign metrics report | CISO |
| Board brief | CISO |

## Post-Click Experience

A landing page must:

- state that this was a simulation
- avoid blame
- identify exact cues
- explain what a real attacker could gain
- give three next-time actions
- show how to report
- finish in 5 minutes or less

## Vishing Ethics

Employees should not be pressured to disclose actual passwords during a simulation. The script may test whether the employee would begin to comply, but the facilitator must stop before a real secret is spoken.
