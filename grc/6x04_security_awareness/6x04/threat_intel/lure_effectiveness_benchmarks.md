# Lure Effectiveness Benchmarks

**Source type:** internal incident review, simulation planning assumptions, healthcare-sector program benchmarking  
**Purpose:** Provide realistic ranges for Task 1 and Task 2  
**Important:** These are planning ranges, not guaranteed outcomes.

## Benchmark Table

| Rank | Lure Category | Mechanism | Healthcare Factor | Expected Click Rate Range | Expected Report Rate Range |
|---:|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | EHR account suspension | Authority + urgency | Blocked EHR access threatens ability to deliver care | 40-62% | 8-18% |
| 2 | Secure patient message | Familiarity + urgency | Staff routinely receive patient-context alerts | 34-50% | 10-22% |
| 3 | MFA/compliance enrollment | Familiarity + authority | Staff expect compliance tasks and remote-access changes | 25-42% | 15-30% |
| 4 | HR benefits deadline | Scarcity + urgency | Personal financial consequence increases action bias | 22-38% | 18-35% |
| 5 | Vendor emergency support | Authority + urgency | Vendors are normal part of healthcare operations | 18-35% | 20-40% |
| 6 | Executive/BEC request | Authority + social proof | Lower click rate but high impact if action taken | 8-22% | 25-45% |

## Behavioral Interpretation

A lower click rate does not always mean lower risk. Executive and finance lures may produce fewer clicks but can cause high-dollar or high-sensitivity outcomes. Clinical lures may produce high click rates because of workflow pressure and shift timing.
