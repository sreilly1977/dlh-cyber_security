## Your biggest threats

- **Wire fraud by impersonation.** A message that reads like it is from the CFO or a fellow executive, requesting a confidential payment or document action before a deadline — three peer-organization executives were targeted with near-identical scenarios in Q1 2026. It targets you because your signature moves money and your requests are rarely challenged ("if a director asks for something, you don't want to slow them down"). **Watch for:** any request combining money, credentials, or confidential documents with urgency and secrecy.

- **Spear phishing while traveling.** Board papers on open airport WiFi, confidential calls within earshot, and login approvals from unfamiliar networks. **Watch for:** authentication or document prompts arriving when you are outside your normal environment and routines.

- **Compromise of your credentials.** Your account can authorize operational damage beyond your inbox: payments, contracts, personnel actions, board communications. **Watch for:** notifications of access from devices or locations that are not yours — and treat MFA prompts you did not initiate as an alarm, not an annoyance.

## What to do right now

- Make independent verification a fixed rule: any request involving money, credentials, or confidential documents is confirmed by voice, using a number from the directory — never a number or reply path contained in the request.
- Ensure your assistant has the same rule: most wire fraud attempts arrive at the assistant, framed as your request.
- Before the next trip, have IT confirm your device encryption, VPN, and MFA enrollment are current.

## When something looks wrong

Call **ext. 4-SECURITY** immediately for anything involving money, credentials, or board materials — before replying to the request. Direct your assistant to do the same without waiting for your availability.

## What not to do

- Do not resolve payment or document requests inside the email thread that delivered them — the reply address can be diverted without any visible sign.
- Do not treat "keep this between us" as a compliance instruction; treat it as the reason to verify.
- Do not approve administrative or financial actions from personal email, even when traveling.
