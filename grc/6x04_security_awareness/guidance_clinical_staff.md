## Your biggest threats

- **Epic account phishing.** Emails claiming your Epic account failed verification or will be suspended "before your next shift." They target nurses and patient care techs because blocked chart access stops your work at shift start. **Watch for:** any email about your Epic login that sends you to a link instead of the Epic window you already use.

- **Fake vendor support calls during equipment issues.** The caller says they need remote access *now* to fix the pump, the imaging system, the interface. It targets you because clinical operations depend on vendors and the middle of a problem is the worst time to interrogate a callback number. **Watch for:** urgency around remote access with no ticket number you can check.

- **USB drops and workstation snooping.** Unknown USB drives left in clinical areas, and people reading your screen at open workstations — on your unit, charts are often visible from hallways. **Watch for:** a drive nobody claims, or an unfamiliar face positioned to see your login or your chart.

## What to do right now

- Bookmark Epic and open it only from that bookmark — logins happen in Epic, never from an email link.
- Save the approved vendor contact list to your phone; hang up on surprise support calls and call back on the listed number.
- Lock your workstation every time you step away (Windows key + L takes one second) and angle screens away from corridors.

## When something looks wrong

Click the **Report Phishing** button in Outlook — it takes under 30 seconds and the SOC sees it instantly. If a call or a message involves patient data or system access and you are unsure, call **ext. 4-SECURITY**.

## What not to do

- Don't enter Epic credentials anywhere except the Epic sign-in you reached yourself.
- Don't plug in a USB drive you didn't receive from IT — hand it to Security instead.
- Don't write off a weird message as "probably nothing" — after clicking, reporting still counts, and you will never be in trouble for a click.
