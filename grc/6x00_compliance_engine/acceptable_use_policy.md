# MedDefense Health Systems — Acceptable Use Policy

**Policy number:** MED-SEC-AUP-001
**Policy title:** Acceptable Use Policy
**Effective date:** 2026-11-01
**Review date:** 2027-11-01 (annual)
**Owner:** IT Security
**Approved by:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
**Classification:** Internal
**Applies to:** All workforce members, contractors, vendors, and temporary clinical staff with access to MedDefense systems

---

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to define how MedDefense systems, accounts, and data may be used, so that patient information stays protected and every workforce member understands the limits of their access. It exists because MedDefense is accountable under the HIPAA Security Rule for what happens on its systems, and because an expectation that is not written down cannot be enforced.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to every person granted access to any MedDefense system, account, network, or data, regardless of device, location, or whether the access is on-site or remote. Specifically, it applies to:

- MedDefense employees, clinical and administrative
- Contractors and consultants working on MedDefense systems
- Vendors and third parties granted remote or on-site access
- Temporary, agency, and locum clinical staff
- Students, residents, and fellows on clinical placement
- Volunteers with system access

Access is granted only after the individual has signed the acknowledgment in Section 10.

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Meaning in this policy |
|---|---|
| MedDefense systems | Any MedDefense-owned or MedDefense-managed computer, server, laptop, mobile device, network, application, email account, or cloud service |
| Patient information | Any information that identifies a patient or could reasonably be used to identify a patient, including medical, billing, scheduling, imaging, and laboratory records |
| Workforce member | Anyone listed in Section 2 |
| Personal device | Any device not owned and managed by MedDefense, including personally owned phones, tablets, and laptops |

## 4. Acceptable Use

MedDefense systems and accounts exist for MedDefense business purposes and authorized clinical care delivery. Nothing in this policy grants a right of personal use, and no user should expect privacy in any MedDefense system, account, or communication.

Authorized use means:

- Using only the accounts issued to you, and only for the work your role requires
- Viewing, entering, or changing patient information only as needed to perform your assigned duties
- Installing software only from the approved software catalogue through IT
- Storing and sharing MedDefense data only in MedDefense-approved locations
- Locking your workstation whenever you step away from it
- Reporting anything that looks wrong, as described in Section 8

MedDefense monitors and logs activity on its systems, including endpoint activity, authentication events, and file transfers. Monitoring exists to protect patients and the organization, not to police individuals, and it is carried out under the controls documented in the MedDefense Endpoint Hardening Baseline and the MedDefense Sigma Detection Catalog.

## 5. Prohibited Activities

The following activities are prohibited without exception. Each one is a terminable offense and may also be a violation of federal law.

1. **Accessing patient records outside the active care relationship for that patient.** Looking up a patient you are not treating, including family members, coworkers, public figures, or your own record, is prohibited. Curiosity is not a business purpose.
2. **Sharing credentials or permitting another person to use your account.** Your username, password, and multi-factor authentication codes are yours alone. Do not write them down where they can be seen, and do not let anyone else log in as you, including a supervisor or a colleague covering your shift.
3. **Installing unauthorized software on any MedDefense endpoint.** This includes browser extensions, utilities, remote access tools, and free or trial software. Software must come from the approved catalogue and be installed by IT.
4. **Connecting personal devices to the clinical network without IT Security authorization.** Personal phones, tablets, laptops, and wearable devices may not connect to the clinical network, and may not be used to photograph, record, or transmit patient information.
5. **Transmitting MedDefense data using personal email, consumer cloud storage, or messaging applications.** This includes personal Gmail, Outlook, iCloud, Google Drive, Dropbox, WhatsApp, SMS, and any similar service. MedDefense email and approved MedDefense file services are the only permitted channels.
6. **Attempting to access systems, data, or network segments beyond your assigned role.** Do not probe, scan, or attempt to reach anything you have not been granted access to, and do not attempt to raise your own permissions.
7. **Entering or processing patient data into AI tools, external web services, or third-party platforms without an approved Business Associate Agreement.** Do not paste patient information into chatbots, translation services, transcription tools, coding assistants, or any other external platform unless IT Security and Legal have confirmed in writing that an executed Business Associate Agreement covers that use.

Additionally prohibited: disabling, bypassing, or interfering with any security control, including antivirus, endpoint detection and response agents, disk encryption, logging, screen locks, and multi-factor authentication; and sharing MedDefense data with anyone who does not have a documented need to know it.

## 6. Patient Information Handling Obligations

You may view, use, or share patient information only when it is needed to do your job for that specific patient, and only the smallest amount needed to complete the task. If you are unsure whether you are allowed to look at, change, or share a piece of patient information, stop and contact IT Security before you act.

## 7. Remote Access

Remote access is a privilege granted for specific job duties, not a general entitlement.

- Remote access must be requested and approved in advance, and is granted only to MedDefense-managed devices.
- Multi-factor authentication is required for every remote session.
- You must connect through the MedDefense VPN before reaching any MedDefense system.
- **Accessing MedDefense systems from public or shared Wi-Fi without an active VPN connection is prohibited.** This includes hotel, airport, conference, coffee shop, and guest networks.
- Do not allow family members, housemates, or visitors to use a MedDefense device or observe patient information on your screen.
- Printing or downloading patient information outside a MedDefense facility requires prior written approval from IT Security.
- Report a lost, stolen, or compromised device immediately, as described in Section 8.

## 8. Reporting Obligations

Report immediately upon discovery, at any hour. Delay is the single largest factor that turns a small incident into a reportable breach.

| What to report | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Suspicious email or unexpected attachment | Prevents phishing and malware spread |
| Unusual system behavior, pop-ups, or slow performance | May indicate active compromise |
| Lost or stolen device, badge, or token | Device may hold patient information |
| Accidental disclosure of patient information | Allows containment and required notifications |
| Suspected credential compromise or unexpected MFA prompts | Indicates account takeover |
| Anything that feels wrong | Early reporting is always the safer choice |

**How to report:** open a ticket in the IT Security queue in the MedDefense Service Desk portal, or call the IT Security Service Desk. The Service Desk number is published on the MedDefense intranet home page. If you cannot reach the Service Desk, escalate to your supervisor and to the on-call IT Security contact listed in the MedDefense Incident Response Playbook Index.

**When to report:** immediately upon discovery. Do not investigate it yourself, do not wait until the end of your shift, and do not wait to be certain. Reporting a suspicion that turns out to be harmless carries no penalty.

## 9. Consequences of Violation

Violations of this policy are handled through the MedDefense Human Resources disciplinary process and may result in corrective action up to and including termination of employment. Access may be suspended immediately while an allegation is investigated.

Contractors, vendors, temporary staff, and volunteers who violate this policy may have their access revoked and their engagement terminated, and the sponsoring department will be notified.

Some violations are not only policy breaches but potential violations of federal law. Knowingly obtaining or disclosing individually identifiable health information in violation of the HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules can result in personal liability, including civil penalties and criminal prosecution. Following this policy is the primary way to avoid that outcome.

## 10. Acknowledgment and Review

Every workforce member must read and sign this policy before being granted system access, and must re-sign it annually thereafter. Signed acknowledgments are retained by IT Security and are produced as evidence during compliance audits.

IT Security reviews this policy at least once every twelve months and after any significant security incident or change to MedDefense systems. Changes are approved by the CISO and communicated to all workforce members, who must re-acknowledge within thirty days of a material change.
