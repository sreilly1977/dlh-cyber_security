Same evidence. Same scenarios. Same ATT&CK techniques. Two completely different interaction models — one live, one export-based — but the analytical work is the same.

At the end of the week you will have investigated three scenarios through two interfaces, translated Sigma rules into native Wazuh XML rules, built a side-by-side query language comparison that covers jq, Sigma, KQL, and Lucene, written a tool-agnostic investigation playbook, and delivered a bounded vendor evaluation brief that James Chen can put in front of the MedDefense board.

## Why this matters

The SOC industry does not hire SIEM operators. It hires security analysts who can operate any SIEM. Every serious SOC changes its primary platform every three to five years. License costs shift, acquisitions happen, cloud migrations force a platform change, open source licensing terms get rewritten. The analyst who only knows one interface becomes a liability during every transition. The analyst who can look at any query language and decompose it into filters, aggregations, and time windows gets promoted through the transition. That is the difference between a dashboard user and a detection analyst and it is the gap this project exists to close.

There is a second reason that matters more this month. MedDefense is in the middle of a platform evaluation. The board has approved budget for the infrastructure review. Compliance wants evidence that the SOC made a vendor-informed decision, not that they just kept what was convenient. James Chen does not need a spreadsheet of features. He needs an analyst who investigated real incidents on both interfaces and who can defend a recommendation with evidence. That analyst is you this week. Every finding you file, every rule you translate, every click you count, and every command you log becomes part of the brief that ends up in front of Dr. Morales.
